Week 26 / Assessment

Demonstrate your security practice

6 hours core+ 0–6 hours practicePrerequisite: Week 25

A professional portfolio shows decisions, evidence, limitations, and reproducible results. It does not need real customer data or dramatic attack claims. Your final assessment asks you to connect the layers and explain how you know a control works.

Power-on plan: Local staged profiles only; cloud evidence from Weeks 23–25. Cloud resources should already be deleted.

Your week: Session A (2 h): read, prepare, begin the first steps. Session B (2 h): finish the build and tests. Session C (2 h): troubleshoot, write evidence, and complete mentor review. These are planning estimates; use extra hours for repeats. If a gate fails, carry it forward before dependent labs.

Expanded track: See the six security-platform labs and schedule for Elastic, Splunk, and Security Onion. Budget these hours separately from the core; complete their comparison report for the final review.

1. Assemble six small portfolio artifacts

Create a sanitized portfolio folder containing: architecture/power plan; Linux and Windows access-control tests; two detection rule cards and test evidence; one incident report; one backup restore report; and one cloud IAM/network report with cleanup evidence. Use the templates. Include dates, versions, and known limitations in every artifact.

2. Re-run one end-to-end scenario

Mentor selects either a file-change case or the benign Suricata ping case. State expected behavior first. Generate the event, find it locally, trace it centrally, decide its significance, and document a recovery or tuning action. Use fresh timestamps. A replay-only Windows exercise must be labeled replay.

3. Give a 20-minute technical demonstration

Spend 4 minutes on architecture, 6 on the live local scenario, 4 on identity/access tests, 3 on recovery, and 3 on cloud lessons. Keep a backup of screenshots/output in case of hardware trouble, and clearly distinguish live results from recorded ones.

4. Answer the oral assessment

Explain: a browser-to-server request across layers; authentication versus authorization; when a sensor cannot see traffic; the source-to-alert pipeline; one false positive; the difference between containment and recovery; how to prove a restore; and cloud management versus data access. It is acceptable to say “I do not know; here is how I would verify it.”

5. Receive a specific readiness decision

Mentor scores the final work with the rubric. Pass requires at least 8/10, no unresolved boundary/secrets issue, a working detection with positive and negative evidence, a verified restore, and a clear explanation of replay/coverage limits. Record strengths, three improvement areas, and one next role to explore: infrastructure security, detection engineering, cloud security, vulnerability management, or incident response. The course completion is not a professional certification.

6. Plan the next 90 days

Choose one specialization based on the work he enjoyed, not on tool popularity. Schedule one repeatable project, one reading track, and one monthly mentor review. Consider targeted paid practice only for a demonstrated gap. Before publishing any portfolio, remove credentials, tokens, private keys, raw identifiers, and third-party restricted assets. Keep private originals and publish only reviewed copies.

Pass this week

  • Six reviewed portfolio artifacts
  • Fresh scenario and final score
  • A focused next-90-days plan

Explain without the guide

  • Which evidence would convince another engineer your control works?
  • Which parts of your environment remain untested?

If something fails

A demo fails: state the actual failure, locate its layer, and explain recovery. Do not substitute an old screenshot without labeling it. Honest troubleshooting is part of the assessment.

Optional practice · 0–6 extra hours

Teach one Week 1–6 lab to another beginner and record where your explanations fail. Feed those changes into the next course edition.
